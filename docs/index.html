As Marigold Pemberton-Hale quietly catalogued new arrivals at her beloved bookshop, Pemberton Books, she couldn’t help but notice an unusual silence. The normally bustling hub of the village was strangely quiet. The church bells, which usually tolled out with cheerful regularity, had fallen silent for a whole afternoon.

Marigold’s keen eyes caught sight of Reverend Aubrey Finch, who was supposed to be tending to Sunday’s sermon but seemed unusually preoccupied. She discreetly followed him down the narrow cobblestone street towards St. Wilfred's Church. Inside, she found him pacing nervously, his hands clasped tightly together.

"Rev'nd Finch," Marigold called out softly. "Is everything alright?"

Reverend Aubrey jumped at her voice and turned to face her with a look of relief etched on his weathered face. "Ah, Marigold! I was just thinking. It's been ages since we had such a quiet day."

Marigold nodded, trying to keep the curiosity out of her tone. "And what has you so worried?"

Reverend Aubrey sighed and leaned against the pew. "You know how much the villagers love a good mystery? Well, there’s this old manor on the outskirts that hasn’t been used for years. The previous owner, Sir Cedric Blackwood, left quite the legacy behind."

"Sir Cedric Blackwood?" Marigold repeated, her mind racing with possibilities. "What kind of legacy?"

Reverend Aubrey shook his head. "That’s what I’m trying to find out. People say he was always secretive about the contents of his manor. Some say he had a curse on it, others that he was hiding something valuable."

Marigold's eyes narrowed as she took in Reverend Finch’s nervousness. She knew better than anyone how easily gossip spread in Willowbrook-on-Fen.

"Have you spoken to Mrs. Vance about this?" Marigold asked, her voice tinged with a hint of authority.

Reverend Aubrey looked at her sharply. "You know who she is?"

Marigold nodded, remembering the unofficial mayor of Willowbrook opinion. "Yes, I do. She’s like the wind in the village – always blowing around and hearing everything."
